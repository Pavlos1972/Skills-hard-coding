# The two reads of the transcript (the reading lock)

**Read the whole transcript, start to finish, at least twice before drafting.** The program enforces it: it serves the transcript in numbered chunks (`tx.py chunk`, 400 lines or fewer each), refuses to skip one, and will not serve the next chunk until you have recorded what this one adds (`run.py ledger add`) or said in your own words why it holds nothing to record (`run.py ledger ack`). Pass 2 is refused until Pass 1 has served every chunk. A quote that is not really in the transcript is refused the moment you type it. `tx.py scan` is the keyword scan: it runs in addition to the two reads, never instead of them, and every decision, change or request word needs a ledger entry within 3 lines or a recorded review.

**What the ledger holds here:** what the call adds to the form's facts: a gap the call fills, a difference from the form, a contact detail, a quote request (insurer and New Business or Servicing), a next step or appointment, a change of mind. The final state governs: if something is changed later in the call, the later statement wins and the earlier one is not shown as current.

**Several transcripts for one meeting:** `run.py join` puts them in one file, each part marked by a separator line; `tx.py prep` then checks every part on its own (duration, gaps, closing marker). At each join you check that the last exchange of one transcript and the opening of the next pick up the same topic or figure and record it (`run.py continuity`); a real gap is flagged to Paul, never filled in silently.

**Garbled or unclear speech:** if a line is unclear, garbled or ambiguous, do not turn it into a fact: flag it to the user. Digit-by-digit read-outs (ID, phone, tax number) are transcribed exactly once the client has confirmed them, and are checked against the quote digit for digit.

**Speaker labels:** the recording may label both spouses with the same name. Attribute every statement from its content (who owns the policy, whose job, "my husband / my wife") and from the form's file, never from the label. Where a statement cannot be attributed with evidence, do not guess: flag it in the notes of both sets and to Paul.

**Lines that carry a request.** Besides the scan's decision, change and request words, the checker treats these as request lines. HARD words: FNA, quote, shortfall, RGAR, EGAR, goal analysis, reduced, authority, payslip, appointment, next meeting, set up, follow up, credit life (also creditor life and life cover on the bond). SOFT words: ask, request, sign, can you, could you, would you, please, I'd like, I will need, organise, arrange, book, prepare, run the numbers, come back to me, get back to me, send me, email me, let me know. A request line counts as handled only when an entry of a kind that must be covered (not a plain `fact` or `figure`) sits on it, or sits within 3 lines and shares a topic word of 4 letters or more with it (a request verb or filler such as send, email, ask, sign, need, please, would does not count). A soft line may instead be dismissed with `run.py ledger review --line N --reason ...` where the reason quotes some of the line's own words. A hard line can be set aside only on Paul's recorded answer (`run.py ask --need dismissals --about "<line numbers>" ...`, then `run.py answer`): the question text must name at least 4 of the words (4 letters or more) of every line it names, one answer frees at most 5 lines, and the glance list shows the full question and his words next to each freed line. An entry on a quote line (FNA, quote, shortfall, RGAR, EGAR, reduced) must be kind `quote_request`. It is covered only by the quote row or request field of the product its line names (a shortfall row cannot cover an FNA line). A line that names TWO products (an FNA and a shortfall quote) needs a cover for EACH from that product's own row or request field, and a request field answered `N/A` or `Not discussed` covers nothing. `Not discussed`, `N/A` and `not on the call` (and `Requested`) are all refused on that product's request, type or insurer field while the request is in the ledger. A credit-life line is a hard line too, and only a fill or an answer of a credit-life field (cl_req, cl_lender, cl_bal, cl_life, or a debt's `Credit life with lender`) covers it; `Requested` is refused while the call mentions the field, unless a reason cites that line and quotes it. The VALUE a fill or an answer writes is the chat's own reading of the call: the glance list prints it for every covering item (`fna_req = No`, a quote row's insurer and basis, the first 40 characters of a note) and in one line, `Values written to cover the call`. The program refuses a bare No (or None) as the cover of a request, quote or next-step line that asks for something (a request verb such as ask, request, send, quote, will need, would like, can you or please in its window, and no negation such as no, not, don't, never, none, without, decline or cancel), requires `Yes` from a request field that covers a quote line, and refuses `No` for a credit-life field when the sentence it cites (or, for a question, the answer after it) says credit life exists. A `No` with a negation on the line is accepted. If the client really said no, ask Paul about that field (`run.py ask --kind knowledge --need <name> --about <field id> ...`, then `run.py answer`): his words must say no, and they are then shown next to the cover. The program cannot know what the client really said. A request line cannot be recorded as a plain fact, also one or two lines away, and an entry cannot be set aside as plain talk. If more than a quarter of the call or more than 60% of these lines are dismissed, ask Paul (`run.py ask --need dismissals ...`). A request worded with none of these words is not required by the program.
