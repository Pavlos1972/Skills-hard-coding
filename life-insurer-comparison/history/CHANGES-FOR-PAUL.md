## What the skill does now, in plain words
- A program walks the chat through 12 steps and refuses to skip one: find the documents (computer, then Microsoft 365, then Zapier, every try logged), read them page by page, settle the scope from the client's own notes, fetch and save each insurer's own guide, enter the figures, let the program work out the premium escalation, show you ONE confirmation table and ask ONE question, build the letter, save it, draft the covering email, message Sitamah, final audit.
- **The benefit scope is proved, not guessed:** every category (life, severe illness, disability, income protection, debt cover, childcare) needs a line of the client's own notes as evidence, or your own words. The program shows the notes' lines that mention each category and marks the ones with "removed", "excluded", "budget" and similar words. A category that is out appears nowhere in the letter or the email: the program scans both for the category's words (the old check had five words; debt cover, childcare and plain "disability" are new).
- **Every figure is checked:** the chat enters each figure with the exact line of the document it came from. The program proves the line is in the document's text and the figure is in the line. A premium pattern (fixed or age-related) must come with the document's own words; if the words and the type disagree the program flags it.
- **The program does the sums:** every year-on-year percentage from the printed rand figures, which year of the table the "current premium" is (the anchor), the outliers (a year about double its neighbours, or negative), a printed percentage that disagrees with the printed rand figures, a table shorter than 10 years ("not provided"), a guarantee period the document does not state.
- **The letter is drawn from one fixed template** (the old look: gold for Wealth Revolution, a colour per insurer by position, a dark card for the proposal, charcoal totals rows, Playfair Display and Source Sans with fallbacks). Header, Key Facts Banner, salutation, the sections you ticked in the fixed order, the signed closing, Disclaimer and Sources. Sections default to Premium Snapshot, Cover Comparison, Premium Escalation, Summary and Verdict and Sources; Loyalty only when a quote has one; Definition Quality only when you want it. The covering email is plain paragraphs and 4 to 6 bullets with no typed signature.
- **Nothing is sent without your yes.** The letter goes to you in the chat and is saved in the client's Comparative Quotes (never overwriting without your yes). The Outlook draft is made with the Microsoft 365 draft tool (To the client, Cc Sitamah, never sent by Claude), read back and checked; then the Teams message goes through Zapier. The reply to you is the short audit: one line per check with a tick or a cross.

## Fixed while building (nothing for you to decide)
- The first year of the escalation table has no percentage badge (there is no earlier year to compare it with); every later year with a rand figure has one, printed or calculated, and the footnote names which insurer's are which. The old text said "every year" but could not mean the first.
- The old text said each insurer's "row" in one step and "column" in another: it is a column per insurer.
- The old text's circular order (scope before the documents, documents before the scope) is now one driver order.
- The intake notes are looked for in the client's `2. Fact Find` folder first (that is where the Teams and Fact Find skills save them now), then the main folder.
- Product and file names in the letter's header and in Disclaimer and Sources are facts and are not scanned for out-of-scope words; everything else is.
- The old rule "Paul's own stated industry baseline" is dropped (your answer 5). The program compares a year with the same insurer's neighbouring years and with the other insurers.

## What a program cannot prove (still your eye or your judgement)
- That each saved insurer guide is the real, current page it says it is (the chat fetched and saved it; the program checks every statement's line is in the saved text and records the address and date).
- Whether a feature really is exclusive to one insurer, who wins, whether the Verdict is fair, whether the Key Facts Banner is specific to this client, whether the plain English is plain; the advice itself. The program shows all of these on your table before the build and checks only that each "wins" badge rests on a sourced statement.
- That the scope reading is right: the program proves the cited line is in the notes, not that it means what the chat says.
- Figures read from a page image (a scanned schedule): shown to you with the page number and listed as attested.
- That each evidence line is the right line of its document for its figure.
- The Outlook draft and the Teams message themselves: the program builds the exact text and checks the read-back, but cannot see Outlook or Teams, and cannot attach the file. How the letter looks in Chrome.
- **Not yet proven on a real client.** I have never seen a real BrightRock, Discovery, Momentum, Liberty or Old Mutual quote or schedule, or a real insurer guide. The checks work on the lines the chat copies from the text the program shows, so a layout I did not guess should be accepted; the fetching of the guides from the web (the search and fetch tools, then Claude in Chrome) is unproven until one real try. The first real run may need one fix.
