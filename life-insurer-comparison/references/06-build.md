# Step 8: The letter (built by the program from the fixed template)

**Format.** One standalone HTML file that opens cleanly in Google Chrome and can go to the client as a deliverable; a PDF copy is made only when Paul asks. It reads as a letter written to the client: the client is always "you" ("your policy", "you will receive"). Side-by-side comparisons are tables with labelled totals rows sourced from the documents.

**Structure (fixed order).** Header (client, the policies with reference numbers, the date, the adviser) then the Key Facts Banner (a gold-highlighted box) then the salutation ("Dear [First name]," and a warm opening line in the chosen voice) then, only if ticked and only for in-scope categories: Premium Snapshot, Cover Comparison (with the second life and "What is different"), Premium Escalation (at least 10 years), Loyalty / Payback / Bonus (the one small table), Definition Quality, Summary and Verdict (a dark box with bullets, each a bold headline then supporting detail), then the signed closing (Paul's name, Wealth Revolution (Pty) Ltd, FSP 45946, WhatsApp, office, email), then Disclaimer and Sources (every source document with its number and date; the calculated projections are illustrative, not guaranteed, unless the insurer's own table says otherwise; any general product materials are named as such).

**Look.** Neutral and insurer-agnostic: colours go by position (the order the insurer was introduced), never by brand; up to six. The house gold is Wealth Revolution's and is never given to an insurer. Totals rows are charcoal; the proposal gets the dark lead card, every other insurer a light card in its own colour; headings in Playfair Display, body in Source Sans 3 (with fallbacks). The summary cards wrap (2 to 6 insurers); a table wider than the page scrolls sideways with the header row and the first column kept in view.

**Checked by the program after the build:** the sections are exactly those ticked, in order; the header, banner, salutation and closing are in place; no word of an out-of-scope category appears in the letter or the email (product and file names in the header and the Sources are facts and are not scanned); the voice holds (no third person about the client, no institutional phrase in Paul's voice, no first person in the institutional voice); no quote-version history; every premium and cover amount is in the letter; a percentage badge sits on every year with a figure after the first, gaps read "not provided", the footnote names printed and calculated; tables, rows, lists, sections and divs open and close; no script, no image, no outside link except the fonts. A human glance (attested): how it looks in Chrome, whether the plain English is plain, whether the banner is really specific to this client.
