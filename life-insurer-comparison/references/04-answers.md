# Step 5: The answers (answers.json)

Every figure is entered WITH a verbatim line of the document it comes from (`evidence`). The program proves the line is in the document's text and that the figure is in the line. `source` is the document key (for example `quote_samplelife_quote`; the program prints the keys); it can be left out when it is the side's own document. A line on an image page: `"image_read": true, "page": N`.

**What to extract for each side** (only what is genuinely present and material; never a figure for a category outside the scope): the policy or proposal number and start date; the monthly premium (total, with riders, add-ons and fees); the yearly premium increase rate and type; the yearly benefit increase rate; the life cover amount; the disability, severe illness and income protection amounts and definition sets (ONLY if in scope); debt cover components (each debt separately with term and cover option, where structured that way); childcare benefits (per child where structured that way); any cashback, loyalty or bonus benefit and all its conditions; any cover increase or buy-up facility and its limits; any conversion or restructuring facility; whether a spouse has independent cover or is a rider; health or lifestyle loadings (percentage and unloaded premium). Where the document states a total for lump-sum cover use that figure, do not sum components. **Include only where applicable:** the terminal illness benefit (always in scope); standard exclusions where materially relevant; claims-paying reputation where researched and citable. **Deliberately not on the list:** waiver of premium, underwriting basis, cession. Where a Cash Conversion or guaranteed-value benefit exists, separate what is illustrated (projected) from what is contractually guaranteed and say when a guaranteed floor crystallises.

**Accuracy (non-negotiable).** Every figure from a retrieved document. Never invent a total: only totals as the document presents them. A figure that cannot be verified does not appear ("not available in source documents"). Never combine figures from different quote or schedule versions. Premium projections come from the insurer's own table where one exists. Do not remove or alter a figure that looks anomalous without telling Paul. When a client fact changes mid-build (a new start date agreed after a signed document) say plainly that the signed document says something different, change the answer once and run the steps again (the program rebuilds every place). Flag once, briefly, that figures anchored to the old date should be reconfirmed with the insurer.

**The loyalty table (Section 4) is ONE small table:** Insurer / Benefit (its name) / Amount or % / When, one row per insurer. "Amount or %" is whichever the document states (a rand figure or a percentage); if a benefit is not confirmed on this quote the cell reads "Not on this quote", nothing more. "When" is one short phrase (an upfront discount's duration or expiry, or a payout's date or trigger). Never a conditions column, a forfeiture column, a guarantee-period column, a dual-mechanism breakdown, an "Honest Position" box or a paragraph; at most the one closing line the program adds. If a mechanism has more than one component pick the single figure most relevant today (usually the upfront one) and leave the rest for Paul in conversation. Never lead the banner or the verdict with a payout figure as the reason to switch.

**Unique features (Section 2, "What is different").** Every insurer has genuine differentiators (a proprietary claim-assessment mechanism or broader trigger, survival period differences, maximum payout percentage and claim caps, conversion or "change of need" facilities, buy-up facilities without medical underwriting, needs-matched against fixed-block structuring, a benefit exclusive to one insurer). Each is verified against the research, never assumed from familiarity, and written with a plain-language line on what it means for the client. A green "wins" badge where genuinely advantageous; "tradeoff" (amber) for a fair trade-off; never a badge on an inference. "Exclusive" needs a checked source for every OTHER insurer. Tier-restricted benefits say so. The program adds the "who is ahead" line from the badges.

**Definition quality (Section 5, only when ticked).** This is the section most likely to carry more detail than the client needs: keep it as lean as the loyalty table (the definition set's name and the one material difference). Only the categories in scope. State the definition set for permanent disability and severe illness on every policy where those are in scope, whether severe illness is accelerated or standalone, and where one insurer's definitions are materially broader or narrower, say so with supporting detail. Explain in plain language that broader, more client-friendly definitions generally increase the likelihood of a successful claim.

**Prose** (banner, opening, verdict, features, email): short and plain; the client is "you"; where a cell or note conveys more than two or three facts use a short list, not a dense paragraph. The Key Facts Banner is the single most important issue for THIS client, never generic. Limits are in `assets/settings.json`.
