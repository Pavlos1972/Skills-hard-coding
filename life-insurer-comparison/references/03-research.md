# Step 4: Product knowledge research (every time, for every insurer)

This step is non-negotiable however many insurers are compared. For EACH declared insurer fetch and SAVE the text of its own guide (web search and fetch tools first, then Claude in Chrome; log every try with `--purpose research`) and register it: `run.py doc --role guide --label "<Insurer> - <title>" --file <saved text> --route web|chrome --url <address> --fetched-at DD/MM/YYYY`. The program records the address and the date and checks later that every statement taken from a guide quotes a line that is really in the saved text. It cannot prove the saved text is the real, current page: that stays attested.

- **Exact product name and tier.** Insurer ranges are frequently tiered (for example Premier / Classic / Express) and features on one tier do not apply to another. Confirm the exact product name and tier as stated in the source document, and never assume a feature is included without checking it applies to that specific tier.
- **Authoritative sources only.** Read the insurer's own Technical Guide, product guide, policy wording and official product pages. Handwritten RAR notes and adviser summaries are descriptive only. Where sources conflict, the insurer's own Technical Guide or policy wording wins.
- **Always research and verify before stating anything about it:** the survival period on severe illness benefits and whether it varies by condition; whether severe illness cover is accelerated (reduces life cover on a claim) or standalone; the disability definition set used (exact name and what it covers); the income protection waiting period and benefit term; whether any cashback, loyalty, bonus or premium-return benefit is optional or included, and its conditions; **whether the yearly premium increase is fixed/contractual or age-related/age-rated, taken from the insurer's literal wording on that specific client's document, never from the size of the percentage or from familiarity with the insurer**; the premium guarantee period; the maximum payout percentage on severe illness benefits and whether multiple or unrelated claims are capped; any cover increase or buy-up facility without new medical underwriting and its limits; whether a severe illness payout is a flat percentage or severity-tiered; whether a loyalty mechanism has more than one moving part (an upfront discount plus a separate engagement-linked adjustment); whether an upfront discount is time-limited (a promotional / "New Business Special Offer" rate) or standing. Only the categories in scope matter.
- **Never state without confirmation:** a sub-product, rider or tier name not in the source document; an optional rider as included unless the source includes it; a "broader trigger" for income protection or disability unless explicitly confirmed; a temporary or partial disability layer unless confirmed.
- **Flag everything unconfirmed.** Anything based on general product knowledge rather than the client's own documents is a statement with `basis: "inference"`, which the letter shows as "To be confirmed with [Insurer]" ("Based on the [Insurer] [Tier] product range, to be confirmed against the actual policy schedule").
- **A feature that is not on the client's document** but is material to their stated concern (for example retrenchment or instalment protection) may be surfaced if it is within the scope, taken from the insurer's own public materials, marked `not_on_proposal` with the conditions the client would need to meet, and listed in the Sources as coming from general product materials.
- **The yearly increase pattern.** Read the document's own words. A flat stated rate printed as one contractual figure ("Yearly premium increase: 10.0%" or "Compulsory 10.00% / Voluntary 0.00% / Total 10.00%") is fixed even though 10% might feel age-rated in size. "Age factor", "Age Rated Premium Pattern" or a rate that recalculates against the client's current age at each review is age-rated however small the first figure looks. Never describe an age-related pattern as fixed, and never a stated-fixed pattern as age-rated.
- **Moving parts.** Understand a loyalty mechanism fully even though the letter shows only the single most relevant figure (usually the upfront one).
