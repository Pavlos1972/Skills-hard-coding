## Flaws found
Contradictions with other skills and inside the skill:
1. **The skill never says what the document looks like.** It says "follow the visual/tone conventions of `life-insurer-comparison`" (twice) and "the covering email". Format (HTML? PDF?), palette, letter shape and email wording all live in the other skill (a 1,076-line file: HTML letter, a positional insurer palette with a house gold, a companion email). Change that skill and this one changes silently; there is no template here.
2. **The board and this skill disagree about the hand-over.** The board says "auto-draft the client-facing proposal ... HTML body, straight to Outlook Drafts. To [Client], CC Sitamah". This skill only "hands it to Paul in the chat": no file name, no save in the client's folder, no Outlook draft, no message to Sitamah. (The three proposal skills already have all of that.)
3. **TFSA limits contradict `om-tax-free-skill`.** Step 6 says annual R36,000 and lifetime R500,000; the Tax-Free skill (same 14/10/2026 build) has R46,000 and R500,000. One of the two is wrong for 2026/27. Not decided by me.
4. **Wrong cross-references:** Step 0 item 7 sends you to "the standard four-table document (see Step 8)" and Step 2 to "the summary comparison table (Step 8, item 1)"; the four tables are Step 7 and Step 8 is the pre-delivery check.
5. **Step 6a contradicts Step 7.** 6a says a disagreement between a supplementary document and the quote must be named "in the document's opening notes"; Step 7 says four tables and "no extra narrative sections bolted between them". There is no opening-notes section.
6. **The as-at date:** Step 4 says note the fact sheet's date "directly under the table" and Step 7 says "once, directly under the table", but the funds come from different providers with different dates: one date cannot be true for all of them.
7. **Table 1 versus Table 2:** Table 1 lists "initial commission, ongoing commission"; Step 2 puts "ongoing advice fee" in Table 2 and says initial fees go in Table 1. The ongoing commission may be shown twice or in the wrong table.
8. **Step 0 asks for what the file already holds (S3):** the client's risk profile "ask the adviser if it is not in this conversation" (it is in the client's Fact Find). Eight setup questions, several of them "usually readable off the quotes, only ask if unclear".
Nothing forces the rules (text only):
9. **Every fee figure is typed by the chat** from the quote, and the "Total ongoing fees" row is added up by hand. Nothing proves a figure is in the quote, that the total is right, or that the quote showed a fee breakdown and not only an EAC.
10. **Apples-to-apples (Step 1) is a rule with no check:** product type, contribution structure, term and investment amount must match across the quotes; nothing compares them.
11. **The live fund fact sheet research (Step 4) is mandatory but unprovable:** benchmark, target, 1/3/5-year performance and the as-at date are typed from the web, with no record of which page, when, or what it said. "More than a quarter stale" is judged by eye.
12. **The pre-delivery check (Step 8) is 11 items done by eye:** no EAC headline, a plain-language intro above every table, every industry term (TER, TIC, EAC, commission types) explained at first use, the four tables in order, nothing from a previous client's comparison. Most are checkable by a program (structure, terms, figures in the quote); judging plain language is not.
13. **The supplementary document rule (6a) cannot be enforced:** "never silently merge" and "name each difference" depend on the chat noticing.
14. **TFSA limit check (Step 6):** the remaining annual and lifetime room is a sum of figures from the Fact Find or statements, plus the quote's contributions: arithmetic done by hand, with the limits typed into the skill (stale risk).
Vague or missing:
15. **"A Rand-per-month conversion at the client's real contribution level"** has no method. A fee percentage applies to the fund value, which grows, not to the contribution, so a Rand figure is only meaningful on a stated fund value. The verdict ("grounded in the fee-total difference from Table 2") has no stated way to measure the difference.
16. **Only RA and TFSA have tax rules (Step 6).** Endowment (tax inside the fund) and living annuity (drawdown rate) are named in the description and get nothing: the same four tables, no tax or product-specific rules.
17. **No access routes, no run state, no log (S4, S8):** the quote documents and the fact sheets are read however the chat likes; nothing is logged; no reading lock for long quote PDFs.
18. **No data file, no checker, no tests, no packaging.** The fixed look is described, not built.
Standards and clutter:
19. About 25% of the 431 lines are the copied universal rules (rule 1 "re-load the skill" and rule 9 "propose_skills" are obsolete: S7), the Delivery gate, a hand-typed stamp and changelog (S2) and a pointer to a "Project doc" changelog that is not in the repo.

## What a program CAN and CANNOT do here (honest)
- CAN: force the steps; serve each quote page by page; check every fee percentage, premium, term, fund name and allocation against the quote's own text (exact, next to its label); add up the fee totals; compare product type, contribution structure, term and amount across the quotes and stop on a mismatch; refuse a quote that shows only an EAC; check every fund figure (benchmark, target, 1/3/5-year, as-at date) is in the fund sheet text the chat SAVED, and the as-at date against today (stale after one quarter); compute the TFSA room left and flag a breach; list every difference between a supplementary document and the quote and refuse to finish until each is shown; build the four tables from one data file in a fixed look; check the built file (four tables in order, an intro line above each, no EAC headline, every industry term explained or absent, a date for every fund, nothing left over from another client); derive the file name; check the Outlook draft and Teams read-backs; print a short audit.
- CANNOT: do the web search itself (the chat searches and saves the page text; the program cannot prove the saved text is the real, current page); judge whether a fund suits the client's risk profile, whether the plain language is plain, whether a booster explanation is clear enough or whether a verdict is fair; read a provider's quote layout it has never seen (I have seen no real Sanlam, Momentum or Allan Gray quote: the first real run may need one fix); read scanned pages (the chat views them and it stays attested).
