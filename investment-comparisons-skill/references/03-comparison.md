# The comparison: apples-to-apples, tax, flexibility and the optional parts

`run.py check` works this out from your answers. Nothing here is typed by you.

**Apples-to-apples (Step 1 of the old skill).** Do not build a comparison table until all of these match across every quote: the **same product type** (RA with RA, TFSA with TFSA, endowment with endowment, living annuity with living annuity: never an RA against a preservation fund or a TFSA against a discretionary investment, because the tax treatment alone makes that misleading: the program checks each quote's text says it is the product); the **same contribution structure** (recurring premium with recurring premium, lump sum with lump sum: if one is a lump sum and the other a recurring premium, say so explicitly and do not force them into the same fee table); the **same term or retirement age** where the product is term-dependent; and the **same investment amount** (admin and platform fees are usually tiered by Rand value, so quotes made at different amounts have fee percentages that are not directly comparable: ask for quotes at the same amount, or label the mismatch rather than silently comparing tiered percentages side by side). The program compares the contribution structure, the amount, the yearly increase and the term. A mismatch STOPS the run unless Paul says to go on: the one question at the confirmation table then asks him, and the email labels the mismatch in its "Things to know" box. If a mismatch cannot be resolved by asking Paul, do not proceed with a table that implies a false equivalence.

**A lump sum.** If a lump sum forms part of any quote, find out whether it is a Section 14 transfer or voluntary funds (it affects whether a separate lump sum quote is also needed). This is usually readable off the quotes; ask Paul only if it is unclear.

**Fees and the totals.** Every fee is a separate line from the quote's own fee schedule. The asset management fee shown is the blend of the funds weighted by their allocation; "Total ongoing fees" adds up the figures AS SHOWN, so the client's own addition checks out. Where a provider shows a fee another does not, the cell says "Not on this quote": it is never filled in or estimated. A percentage fee applies to the value of the client's fund each year, not to the contribution, so the email shows percentages only. A Rand figure appears only in the optional fees-only comparison, on a fund value Paul names, and is always labelled as an example. If one provider's fee schedule is tiered by Rand value, say so in `flags`.

**Tax treatment (Step 6) — only when Paul asks for the tax note (`extras.tax_note`).** State it explicitly and never let it imply a fake advantage.
- **RA comparisons:** tax deductibility (27,5% of remuneration, capped at the current SARS annual limit) is identical in law across every provider. The note says so, so the client does not infer that one provider's RA is "more tax efficient" than another's: the deduction is a function of SARS rules, not of the product.
- **TFSA comparisons:** the program checks the client's remaining annual and lifetime contribution room against what each quote proposes in its first year and flags clearly in the "Things to know" box if a quote would push the client over either limit. The contributions already made come from the Fact Find or the client's existing TFSA statements: never assume the limits are untouched. **The limits in `assets/tax_facts.json` are NOT yet confirmed by Paul** (the old skill said R36,000 a year and the Tax-Free skill R46,000, with R500,000 for life): until he confirms them a Tax-Free comparison STOPS and asks him; they are then set there, in Claude Code, never typed by the chat.
- **Endowment and living annuity:** there are no approved tax or product rules for them. Keep the four tables; if the tax note is asked for, the program stops and asks Paul what it should say (nothing is invented).

**Flexibility and contract terms (Step 5) — only when Paul asks (`extras.flexibility`).** Whether contributions can be increased, decreased or paused without penalty; the transfer-out terms (Section 14 friction, exit penalties on older-style non-LISP contracts); the ad hoc or additional lump sum allowance. Each item comes from the quote (a phrase of it is checked).

**A fees-only comparison (`extras.verdict`) — only when Paul asks.** Never anything but the fee table's totals: it lists each provider's total ongoing fees, names the cheapest and the difference in percentage points a year, and, if Paul named an example fund value, the Rand difference on that value, labelled as an example. It is never grounded in EAC, past fund performance or a loyalty bonus figure, and it says it is not advice on which fund will perform better.

**Which extras.** Apples-to-apples flags, the tax note, flexibility and terms and a fees-only comparison are added only if Paul asks (or, for flags, because something genuinely needs flagging); otherwise the four tables are the complete, simple deliverable.
