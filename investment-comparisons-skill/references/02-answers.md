# The answers file

Write ONE JSON file, every answer with its source. The program checks each fee, premium, term, fund, allocation, loyalty figure and fund-sheet figure against the text of the right document and refuses what it cannot find. **Ask Paul only for what the documents cannot tell you:** which extras he wants (tax note, flexibility, a fees-only comparison and its example fund value), the meetings already held (pre-fill them from the file names) and the next meeting. The client's name, product type, contribution structure, risk profile and the lump sum (if any) are read off the quotes and the Fact Find, not asked.

- `product_type` is `RA`, `TFSA`, `Endowment` or `Living Annuity`, the same for every quote (the program checks each quote's text says so). `tfsa` only for a Tax-Free comparison; `sources.tfsa` then too; `sources.supplement` when a supplementary document is registered.
- **Fees (Step 2 of the old skill).** Pull EVERY fee out as a separate line item from the quote's own fee schedule, never a blended cost. The standard rows are: asset management fee (entered per fund; the program blends it by allocation), net administration fee, ongoing advice fee, adviser remuneration account charge; the total is added up by the program. **"Net administration fee" is the line most likely to be doing quiet, heavy lifting** (it has been as low as about 0.5% and as high as about 3% between providers): never assume it is minor; always take the figure from the quote. Use the quote's OWN fee labels where they map onto those rows; if a provider labels or splits fees differently (separate initial and ongoing platform fees, a fund manager TER not folded into the asset management fee), keep its own lines: fidelity to the quote wins over a tidy template. A label that is not in `assets/plain_terms.json` needs a plain explanation in `plain`. Percentages keep their own decimals (0.575% stays 0.575%).
- **Initial fees** (once-off advice or admin fees, initial commission) go in `initial_fees` and appear in Table 1, never in the ongoing-fee table. **Performance fees**, where a fund has one, go in `performance_fees`: they are shown as a note under the fee table and are NOT folded into the total (they vary).
- **Loyalty benefits and bonuses (Step 3).** They are a minor, secondary factor, not the reason to recommend a provider. For each, one line: what triggers it (term completed, no missed premiums, minimum investment period), whether it is contractually guaranteed or discretionary, and when it pays out (maturity or retirement only, or periodic). A mechanism with more than one quoted percentage, or that is not self-explanatory from one line, needs its own plain explanation (`explanation`, 30 to 600 characters) shown before the numbers, saying: how it works mechanically (for example "nothing is added along the way: one bonus is promised at the end and it only pays out when the plan ends"); why more than one percentage is quoted (each yearly increase is a new slice that starts building its own bonus, so a rate on the first contribution and a blended rate on all of them are genuinely different numbers); what it is compensating for when the quote itself says so (a bonus that offsets a specific fee is partly a refund of a cost already priced in: say so plainly, and give a `source_line` that is in the quote); and how it compares in shape, not only in size, with the other providers' mechanisms (paid periodically and resilient to a paused contribution, against paid once at the end and dependent on staying the full course). Never let the opening or any verdict lead with a bonus figure.
- **Fund sheets (Step 4).** For each fund: the fact sheet's own benchmark wording (do not paraphrase it into another benchmark name), the stated target or objective (for example CPI + 4 to 5% or whatever it actually says: never default to "+4-5%" if the sheet says otherwise), the 1, 3 and 5 year performance, annualised and net of fees where the sheet distinguishes gross and net, the sheet's own "as at" date, a one-line description in our own words (25 words or fewer, never copied), and the fund's risk label from the sheet. A fund whose sheet could not be found has the entry `{"provider", "fund", "not_found": true}`. Past performance is supporting context only: never the headline and never a substitute for the fee comparison.
- **Risk fit.** The client's risk profile is read from the Fact Find. The program compares each fund's risk label with it and flags a difference to Paul; whether the fund suits the client is his judgement. Also note, if the quote shows it, whether the client is restricted to the provider's own house funds or has open-architecture access.
- **Supplementary documents (Step 6a).** Treat a provider's second document that disagrees with its formal quote as new information to reconcile, never as an automatic replacement. Never silently merge the two and never average them. Name each specific discrepancy (for example "this projection shows a 3.50% booster, the formal quote states 2.5%") in `supplement_checks` with both values and the supplement's date; note when its underlying assumptions differ (for example a single-fund allocation where the quote split the money across two funds). The email shows every difference in its "Things to know" box, attributed to the document by name and date, and recommends Paul confirms with the provider which figures are current before the client relies on the supplement. The program lists figures that are only in the supplement as a hint; finding the differences is your job and stays attested.
- **Flags** (`flags`): short sentences for Paul that the documents show but the program cannot judge. Never leave a known discrepancy out.
