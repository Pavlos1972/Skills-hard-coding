# Step 4: The analysis (analysis.json)

Every client figure is entered WITH a verbatim line of the document it comes from (`evidence`); the program proves the line is in the document's text and the figure is in the line. `source` is the document key (the program prints the keys) and can be left out when it is the policy's own document. A line on an image page: `"image_read": true, "page": N`. Every product statement is a **statement**: `{"text", "basis", ...}` with `basis` = `"document"` (plus `evidence`), `"product"` (plus `sources: [{"source": <guide or source key>, "evidence": <verbatim line>}]`: two sources, one guide and one independent), `"inference"` (no source; shown "to be confirmed") or `"absent"` (the document is silent; no source). Bullets are 2 to 4 per benefit, one short fact each, never prose.

**Extract for every benefit, one at a time, not just the main life cover:** policy identity (insurer, product and tier, policy or quote number, life assured, owner if different, start or quote date, status as stated, premium payer); the **life cover structure, stated plainly**: term (expires at a set age or date), whole of life (runs for life) or accidental death only (pays only on accidental death): this changes what the client is protected against and is never left ambiguous; every benefit (life, permanent disability lump sum and/or income, dread disease / severe illness / critical illness, income protection, temporary disability, funeral, education, retrenchment, debt cover ...) with its sum assured, accelerated (reduces another benefit on a claim) or standalone, and the definition set used where stated (for example own-occupation against any-occupation); premiums (total, and per benefit if itemised; any health or lifestyle loading and the loaded against base premium where both are shown); waiting periods and survival periods; exclusions and special terms (suicide clause, war, pre-existing conditions, and anything specific: special terms, loadings, underwriting exclusions, often on a separate addendum: check for one); riders and add-ons (included or declined); beneficiaries and proportions; any cover or buy-up facility without new medical underwriting and its limits. **Only what is on the document.** A benefit type that is genuinely absent is "not held": a gap is often exactly what Paul needs to see.

**Dread disease deep-dive (whenever the benefit is held or quoted)**, every topic a statement: **multiple claims** (can the client claim more than once, for unrelated or even related conditions, or is it a single-claim benefit that then falls away?); **payout structure** (100% of the claim amount upfront on a qualifying diagnosis, or severity-tiered, for example 25 / 50 / 75 / 100% by severity: state plainly that a severity-tiered structure is generally a lesser, inferior definition than a flat 100%, because it can pay far less than the full sum assured even on a genuine, serious diagnosis); **duration** (whole of life or expiring at an age); **can it pay more than 100%** (cumulative payouts across multiple or staged claims); **survival period** (the exact number of days if given); **children's cover** (covered, to what amount, on what conditions); **BrightRock Trauma IQ or the equivalent** (for BrightRock: is the client and the children covered under it or not; for others name any directly equivalent needs-matched trauma benefit); **severe illness scope and triggers** (how extensive the condition list is, and whether a severe illness diagnosis triggers a standalone claim or is folded into the main benefit); **accelerated or standalone** (clients very often assume the opposite of what is true). Every item that is materially weaker than what is currently available from the same insurer is a benchmark finding, not a neutral fact.

**Premium pattern and escalation.** Identify the pattern precisely: age-rated (rises as the client ages: the current rate is only a snapshot) or fixed/level (contractually fixed). Never assume either way; confirm from the document's own wording, and if unclear flag it and recommend confirming with the insurer. Break down what drives the increase where the insurer states a formula (for example ACI plus ABI, Annual Contribution Increase plus Annual Benefit Increase, or the insurer's own naming): each component separately and what drives it (CPI, a fixed %, an age-rating table). State the premium guarantee period, and whether a loading changes the pattern. **The 10-year table is built by the program:** the insurer's own printed table if the document has one, otherwise computed from a stated fixed yearly increase (labelled illustrative), otherwise (only if Paul gives an assumption in his own words) from that assumption (labelled "Paul's assumption"), otherwise it reads "not provided". A number is never invented.

**Vitality / wellness mechanics (Discovery and equivalents)** get their own coverage whenever the insurer has a wellness-linked pricing mechanism: how the client's current Vitality status (Blue, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Diamond or equivalent) affects the yearly increase and, where known, the approximate effect; that status depends on annual health assessments and what moves it up or down; how medical aid claims can factor into status; and whether an upfront premium discount was applied at inception, the conditions for keeping it and whether and over how many years it can be clawed back ("premium went up more than expected" is a common surprise). If the insurer has no such mechanism, say so plainly (`applicable: false` with a note): the section is never silently omitted.

**Payback / loyalty (the policy held AND the proposed quote, always).** The specific mechanism, if any (for example Discovery PayBack, BrightRock's Change Moment payout): what triggers it; whether guaranteed or an estimate; what causes partial or full forfeiture or reduction (death before the pay-out date, cancelling, claims that reduce it, any cap tied to premiums paid); standard or an optional add-on with its own premium; and whether the policy held does not actually include a benefit the insurer offers generally (itself a finding). **For the proposed quote the figures come from the insurer's own payback document, never general knowledge and never estimated:** today's total value and the projected value at pay-out; each component (for example retirement, childcare per child) with its amount, pay-out date and how it is paid (lump sum or monthly instalments, for how long, its yearly growth rate); its guarantee period. If the proposed insurer genuinely has no payback on the quote, say so plainly.

**Cession (every time).** Is the policy ceded, and to whom (most commonly a bank or lender as security for a loan, for example a bond)? Check for any cession, cessionary or "security cession" field or endorsement. State plainly what a cession means: while a security cession is in place the cessionary has the first right to the claim proceeds up to the amount owed, before the beneficiaries see anything. If ceded, note whether the cession still looks proportionate to what it was originally securing, framed as worth confirming with the client and the lender, never as a stated fact (the skill cannot verify a loan balance). If not ceded, say "no cession noted on this schedule".

**Second life (every time).** Is there a second life assured on this policy, separate from spousal cover written as a different policy? If yes, is their cover independent or a rider that is entirely dependent on the main life's policy (if it lapses for any reason the second life's cover disappears: easy to overlook)? State the second life's own benefits and sums assured the same way as the main life's. If there is none, say so, and note whether the Fact Find Summary suggests a spouse or partner who might need their own cover (`ff_note`, a For Your Awareness item, never an assumption that they are covered elsewhere).

**Findings.** After the whole picture, identify everything that stands out, specific to THIS document, never a generic list: mistakes on the document itself (inconsistent figures, a definition name that does not match the insurer's real terminology, a total that does not add up, a benefit combination the product does not normally offer); every genuine benchmark finding; anything from the dread disease deep-dive that is materially weaker than what is currently available; a common benefit simply absent (no disability cover on an income earner, no dread disease at all); cover that looks low against the client's income, debt or dependants (using the Fact Find Summary; otherwise say adequacy cannot be fully assessed without it); an active waiting period; an age-rated pattern that will keep climbing where the client may expect it to stay flat (and any Vitality clawback risk); a rider declined at application and never revisited; a special term, loading or exclusion that materially narrows the cover; signs of lapse risk (a grace period or arrears note); an accelerated benefit whose claim would reduce the life cover; a cession; a rider-dependent second life or no cover for a spouse the Fact Find shows; any inconsistency between the document and another client document (mismatched income, marital status). If nothing notable turns up, say so plainly; never manufacture a flag. **Every finding is Action Required** (Paul or the client actually needs to do or decide something, with a real consequence if left alone) **or For Your Awareness** (true and worth knowing but nothing to chase now). **When genuinely unsure, Action Required.**

**The Broker's Honest Opinion (closing section).** A genuine, reasoned, honest read of the product, Claude's own read addressed to Paul as a fellow professional ("my read for you"): the overall assessment in one or two sentences (good, adequate or poor cover for what it is, and why); what is genuinely good (do not manufacture criticism where the product is solid); what would make it materially better, drawing on the benchmark and the Action Required list, as a professional recommendation. It is the one place where Paul gets Claude's actual read, not just the facts.

**Client slides (Step 7), the layout above.** Built FROM the analysis, never from new unchecked figures. Combine same-insurer policies into ONE deck. All four categories always appear (Life, Disability, Dread / Serious Illness, Income Protection) with "Not held" for any the client does not have ("Not held (your choice)" where `left_out` shows the client's own notes say so). **Report 1** (always): title; at a glance; how the policies work in plain English (including the current insurer's cash back or payback); how the cost changes over time; key things to sort out (the Action Required items reworded for the client); where the cover falls short against the client's needs (from the Fact Find). **Report 2** (only with a proposed quote): title; side-by-side table (four categories today against proposed, a Payback row, monthly cost); what the client gains and what to know; the proposed cover in detail; cost comparison over time; a "Paybacks compared" slide; next steps with a timing chip and the quote's validity date. Paybacks are ALWAYS in Report 2: in the table and on their own slide, with the headline figures from the payback document, one short plain line on the key conditions (must be alive at the pay-out date, keep the cover in place, lost if the policy is cancelled, reduced by certain claims, any cap tied to premiums paid), every projected or illustrative figure labelled as an estimate; never a payback as a percentage of premiums unless the insurer's own document says so. Recommendations are brief and simple; the proposed cover is presented as the recommended way forward only when Paul has supplied it as such. Client wording: no Honest Opinion, no internal filing notes, no INTERNAL wording, no internal jargon or benchmark language; reword findings that expose internal record-keeping or Fact Find contradictions as neutral client-friendly actions ("our records did not show them, so we will check they are still active"); every figure from the source documents or the analysis; anything estimated is labelled "estimate" with its assumption; never present an estimate as a contractual figure.
