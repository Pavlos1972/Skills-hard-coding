# Step 3: Product research (mandatory, every time)

**This is what makes the analysis trustworthy rather than a transcription.** A schedule states figures and benefit names but rarely explains what they mean in practice, and schedules do contain typos, outdated tier names or figures inconsistent with the product they claim to belong to.

For EACH declared insurer fetch and SAVE the text of its own current technical guide or product page for the exact product and tier printed on the document (`--role guide`) and, where one exists, an independent source (`--role source`): web search and fetch tools first, then Claude in Chrome; log every try with `--purpose research`. Register each with its web address and the date you read it. The program checks later that every product statement quotes a line that is really in the saved text. It cannot prove the saved text is the real, current page: that stays attested.

- **Confirm what the product actually is,** its normal benefit structure and how its definitions are described. **Cross-check what the document states against what you researched.** Flag any inconsistency explicitly ("schedule states X; [Insurer]'s current [Product] technical guide describes this benefit differently: worth confirming with the insurer").
- **Never let general product knowledge override a client-specific figure** (sum assured, premium, policy number, dates: always from the document). Research verifies terminology, structure and plausibility and feeds the plain-language explanations, the benchmark and the dread disease, premium, Vitality, payback, cession and second life sections: never a substitute for a client figure.
- **Two sources for anything that goes into the benchmark, the dread disease deep-dive, the Vitality or the payback section:** the insurer's own technical guide or product page plus a second independent confirmation. A statement with one source is allowed but is labelled SINGLE SOURCE in the report and on Paul's table. Where sources genuinely disagree, state both and flag the disagreement; do not pick one silently.
- **If the exact product or tier cannot be confirmed online,** say so plainly and rely on what the document states.
- **The benchmark (the heart of the critique).** For every major benefit category held (life, disability, dread disease, income protection) research what the SAME insurer currently offers as its best or more comprehensive version: a higher tier, a broader definition, an additional rider, a less restrictive payout structure. Ask plainly: would this client and their family have been better, more comprehensively covered on a different current option from the same insurer? Raise a benchmark finding only when a genuinely current, real, materially better option exists in that insurer's own range, taken from the saved sources, never from general market knowledge. If nothing better exists say so plainly ("current cover already reflects [Insurer]'s top tier or definition for this benefit"): that is itself a useful, positive finding, not a gap to fill with a manufactured one. If you are genuinely unsure, say so; do not guess. Compare like for like where possible; affordability is a caveat, never a reason to suppress the finding. State in concrete terms what the better option would mean for a claim (for example "would pay out on a broader disability definition", "would cover three severity tiers instead of a flat payout", "would extend cover to the children at no extra underwriting").
