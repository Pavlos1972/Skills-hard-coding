# Step 5: What the program works out

**The premium tables.** For every policy the 10-year table (Year / Client age / Projected monthly premium / % increase on prior year) is the insurer's own printed table, or computed from a stated fixed yearly increase, or computed from Paul's assumption (his words recorded), or "not provided". The program never invents a number: a policy with an age-rated premium and no printed table gets no figures and says so. A computed or assumed table is labelled illustrative on the page and "estimate" on the slides. A printed table with fewer than ten years shows the rest as "not provided". The first year has no percentage because nothing earlier exists.

**The findings.** They are numbered by the program: Action Required first, then For Your Awareness, the same numbers at the top of the report (headline only) and in section 11 (full explanation). A benchmark row with a "better option exists" outcome, a cession and a rider-dependent second life each need a finding behind them (`refs`), so a finding that feeds the tiers cannot be forgotten.

**The sources.** A product statement with two saved sources (the insurer's own guide and an independent one) is "Verified: 2 sources"; with one it is SINGLE SOURCE; an inference is "to be confirmed"; "absent" means the document is silent. The counts are on Paul's table and in the audit.

**The flags** go on the confirmation table: a table that is computed or not provided, a guarantee period not on the document, benefit premiums that do not add up to the document's total (the document's total is used), single-source and inference counts, no Fact Find Summary (adequacy cannot be fully assessed), a proposed quote that has expired or is about to, plus your own.
