# Rules that apply to every step

**What this is.** A critique, not a transcription. The point is to be critical of the policy schedule or quote in front of you, on the client's behalf: catch what it does not say plainly, what looks like a mistake and, most importantly, whether the SAME insurer today would offer this client meaningfully better cover, a better definition or a better structure. A step that only restates the document has not done its job: push on to "is this actually good, and could it be better with this same insurer". It applies equally to an in-force schedule and to a new quote being considered. It applies to any SA insurer and any client; no product facts are hardcoded here: every document is read on its own terms, checked against saved, real product sources, then benchmarked against that insurer's own better options.

**What it produces.** (1) The detailed INTERNAL analysis: one PDF per policy, for Paul only. (2) The client-facing visual summaries: one deck per current insurer (Report 1) and, when a proposed quote exists, Report 2, for presenting on screen. Both are drawn by the program from ONE confirmed analysis file, so nothing is retyped between them.

**What this is not.** Not the comparison of different insurers (that is `life-insurer-comparison`: use it for a replacement or a second opinion across insurers). This skill critiques one insurer's own schedule or quote against itself, one document at a time. The Report 2 slides are a short on-screen summary of a proposal Paul has already decided to put forward; they do not replace a full comparison document.

**Rules**
1. **Say the version first.** The program prints it; say it in exactly those words before anything else.
2. **Source first, never invent.** A client figure is a verbatim line of the client's own document; a product statement is a verbatim line of a saved source (two sources where the page says so). "It seems implied", "it is usual" and "it would make sense" are not sources. If a line is unclear or illegible, flag it ("not stated on the schedule", "illegible: recommend requesting a clean copy"); never guess.
3. **Read before you write.** The program serves every page in order. Searching for keywords is not reading.
4. **Check before you claim.** Never say something is done, saved or working until the program's check or a read-back says so. Say what was checked.
5. **The documents come to you.** Never ask Paul to paste or type figures. Try the computer, then Microsoft 365, then Zapier, and log every try. Ask Paul only when all three failed, saying what was searched.
6. **Never touch another broker's folder.** `Paul Kafentzis (Client Files)` is the only folder under `Company - WR Clients` this skill ever reads.
7. **No tolerance for errors.** A wrong figure, a misdescribed benefit or an invented "better option" in an internal reference document is worse than no document: Paul will trust it and may act on it in front of a client.
8. **A correction re-opens the whole task.** Go back to the source, fix it everywhere, run the checks again and show the table again; the driver re-opens the later steps.
9. **If a rule cannot be met, stop and say so.** Never hand over partial work as if it were complete. Never lower the standard for a frustrated user or a "quick" request.
10. **The program is run as it is.** Never edit a script, the wording or the settings during a run.

**What you never do**
- Fabricate, estimate or carry over a figure from memory, another client or a previous analysis.
- Present the internal analysis as a client document, or put the Broker's Honest Opinion, an internal filing note, the INTERNAL label or internal jargon on a client slide.
- Merge several policies into one detailed internal PDF (one PDF per policy; only the client slides combine same-insurer policies).
- Save a report anywhere but the client's `Analysis & Comparison Reports` folder (not beside the source documents, not the client's folder root, not the chat only).
- Skip the multi-source product research, the benchmark against the same insurer's better current options (it is the core critique and must never be invented), the dread disease deep-dive whenever that benefit is present, the premium table, the Vitality and payback sections (at least "not applicable"), the cession check or the second life check (both every time, even when the answer is "none").
- Assert a loan balance or call a cession disproportionate: it is "worth confirming", never a stated fact.
- Bury the analysis in dense prose where a clean table says it better; write plain-language product explanations as bullet points, never prose; flatten every finding into one list (each is Action Required or For Your Awareness, Action Required when unsure).
- Trim content to achieve a cleaner layout: the Verdict, the Benefit Summary Table and the tiered lists are fast ways in, never a replacement for the full detail.
- Leave the proposed insurer's payback out of Report 2, or build its figures from anything but the insurer's own payback document (if it has not been found, ask Paul for it).
- Build Report 2 when Paul chose "No proposed covers for now" (say plainly in the reply that it was skipped for that reason).

**Working with Paul.** Simple everyday words. One question at a time: the confirmation table is the only question before the build. The reply after the run is the tabulated chat summary and the short audit: one line per check with a green tick or a red cross, one closing line for what is only attested.
